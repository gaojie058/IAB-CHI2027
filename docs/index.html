01 / The questions

A trace is a starting point.
Understanding takes work.

Agents plan, use tools, make mistakes, and recover. People need to understand these actions as they unfold: to check progress, offer a correction, or decide when to take over.

The second Interpreting Agent Behavior (IAB) workshop brings HCI, AI, and social science perspectives together around shared execution and interaction traces. We will compare interpretations, make their evidence visible, and turn unanswered questions into ideas for better methods and interfaces.

01[ a ]

Agents

What do agents do,
and how?

Identify planning, tool use, uncertainty, failure, and recovery. Separate what a trace shows from what we infer.

02[ h ]

Interaction

How do people and agents

Examine instructions, misunderstandings, corrections, and handovers—and the designs that could help repair them.

Coordination → repair

Continuing the conversation This proposed second edition follows the inaugural IAB workshop scheduled for NeurIPS 2026. The CHI edition focuses on interpreting agents in use and designing for human oversight.

02 / Take part

Bring a question.
Build a perspective.

Researchers, designers, practitioners, and students are welcome. You do not need to build models or use a particular analysis tool to contribute.

We welcome work in progress, negative results, concrete cases, and methodological questions. The proposed format brings 25–40 participants, including organizers, together in small facilitated groups.

A

A paper, case, or method

2–4 pages in ACM single-column format, excluding references.

B

An interest statement

Up to 500 words on a relevant experience, question, or perspective.

Planned submission process

Once the call opens, send a PDF to jgao77@jh.edu with the subject “IAB CHI 2027 submission.” Submissions will be non-anonymous and reviewed by two organizers without conflicts for relevance, discussion potential, and a range of perspectives.

Contributions will be non-archival and shared only with author permission. Attendance will follow CHI registration procedures and room capacity, with priority for accepted position-paper authors.

03 / Work together

Read the evidence.
Rethink the design.

Two consecutive in-person sessions, with a conference-scheduled break. We will work in groups of approximately four to six, moving from a shared case to new methods and interface ideas.

Proposed program · 90 minutes per session
Session 01

Interpret & compare

Make the record—and the disagreement—visible.

  1. 00–10

    Welcome & introductions

    Establish the shared task and group roles.

  2. 10–25

    A worked example

    Explore analysis units and the limits of the evidence.

  3. 25–65

    Analyze a common trace

    Annotate actions, human responses, and disputed interpretations.

  4. 65–90

    Compare perspectives

    Identify useful vocabulary and missing context.

Take away Annotated examples + open questions
Session 02

Design & question

Turn interpretation needs into research directions.

  1. 00–10

    Recap & orientation

    Build a common starting point for everyone.

  2. 10–45

    Sketch a method or interface

    Address a concrete need for understanding or oversight.

  3. 45–70

    Exchange, challenge & revise

    Question assumptions, limits, and evaluation plans.

  4. 70–90

    Shape the research agenda

    Prioritize questions and identify follow-up collaborations.

Take away Design ideas + a shared research agenda

Times are minutes from the start of each session, not clock times. CHI determines the final schedule; activities can be adapted to 75-minute sessions.

Beyond the room

We plan to share annotated examples, a record of interpretive disagreements, and a research agenda. Participants will be invited to review a draft synthesis within four weeks of the workshop before any public release.

05 / Before you join

A few practical
details.

Questions about participation or access needs?

Contact the organizers ↗
Do I need a dataset or a particular tool?

No. Organizers plan to provide short, permission-cleared traces, a glossary, and a preparation guide at least one week before the workshop. You may bring a case or method, but neither is required.

How will participation be made accessible?

Activities will support spoken and written contributions, individual reflection before group work, readable digital and printed materials, and paper-based exercises. No paid agent account or live connection will be needed. Please contact the organizers about access needs so support can be coordinated with CHI.

Will participant contributions be published?

Participant contributions are planned to be non-archival and shared on this website only with author permission. Authors may separately use arXiv. If accepted, the workshop proposal itself will appear in the CHI Extended Abstracts proceedings.

Is the workshop confirmed?

Not yet. This is the public information page for a proposed CHI 2027 workshop. Acceptance, the workshop date, participation arrangements, and the final program will be updated here when confirmed. The proposed call is not yet open.
